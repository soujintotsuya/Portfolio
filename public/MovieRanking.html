The Objectively Best Movies of All Time

YOUR soon-to-be Top 5 Movies

Princess Mononoke

Princess Mononoke is a Studio Ghibli film directed by Hayao Miyazaki. It tells the story of Ashitaka, a young prince who is cursed by a demon and travels west to find a cure. On his journey, he discovers a conflict between humans and the spirits of the forest. He meets San, a girl raised by wolves, who fights to protect nature from the iron town led by Lady Eboshi. While San sees humans as destroyers, Ashitaka tries to understand both sides. He hopes to bring peace between people and the forest before everything is lost.

Spirited Away

In its core this movie is about the damage that endless consumption deals to nature, as Chihiro steps into a magical realm in which a bathing house and its running continiously harms "Haku", the personified river spirit symbolizing nature itself. At the same time, it also tells a story about remembering one’s true self and values in a world driven by greed, as Chihiro learns courage, responsibility and empathy while trying to save Haku.

In Time

Time has become currency and the wealthy hoard it making them virtually immortal, while the working class merely lives from day to day. I like this movie because it drastically puts into practice what highly uneven distribution of wealth, even if the picture is a little abstract, actually brings upon the biggest part of society.

Butterfly Effect

In this movie Ashton Kutcher plays a boy/young man growing up, but each time he remembers another detail about his past he wasn't perviously aware of, like it didn't happen, the present also changes drastically, just like the butterfly effect says.

John Wick Series

This movie series isn't deep in any sense, it's just pure action and fighting to please that silent need for revenge in your brain if it emerges from time to time. It is incredibly well choreographed and depicted, but far from that, this list could go well without it.
