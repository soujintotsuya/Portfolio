The Objectively Best Movies of All Time

YOUR soon-to-be Top 5 Movies

Princess Mononoke

A movie by Studio Ghibli and directed by Hayao Miyazaki about human industrialisation, exploitation of nature and natural spirits rising up against them. Amidst this there is a savage woman named San who defends nature accompanied by wolves and a young man by the name of prince Akashita who ventures out to examine what happens to nature and gets drawn towards San and thus starts fighting for nature.

Spirited Away

In its core this movie is about the damage that endless consumption deals to nature, as Chihiro steps into a magical realm in which a bathing house and its running continiously harms "Haku", the personified river spirit symbolizing nature itself.

In Time

Time has become currency and the wealthy hoard it making them virtually immortal, while the working class merely lives from day to day. I like this movie because it drastically puts into practice what highly uneven distribution of wealth, even if the picture is a little abstract, actually brings upon the biggest part of society.

Butterfly Effect

In this movie Ashton Kutcher plays a boy/young man growing up, but each time he remembers another detail about his past he wasn't perviously aware of, like it didn't happen, the present also changes drastically, just like the butterfly effect says.

John Wick Series

This movie series isn't deep in any sense, it's just pure action and fighting to please that silent need for revenge in your brain if it emerges from time to time. It is incredibly well choreographed and depicted, but far from that, this list could go well without it.
